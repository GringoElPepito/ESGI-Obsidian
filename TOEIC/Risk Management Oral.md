The issue is that Two crashes happened in only five month.

First crash was Lion Air Flight 610 crashed into the Java Sea on October 29, 2018, killing all 189 aboard; Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302 crashed near Addis Ababa on March 10, 2019, killing all 157 — 346 dead combined. 
MIT Sloan Cause: MCAS, an automated anti-stall system relying on a single angle-of-attack sensor. When that sensor fed bad data, MCAS repeatedly forced the nose down in a way crews weren't trained to counter. 
Medium Preventable? Yes. Boeing's own engineers and test pilots knew about the MCAS problem before the crashes but withheld it from the FAA, airlines, and pilots, and the FAA had privately estimated MCAS could cause up to 15 crashes over 30 years back in December 2018 — before the second crash. Three decisions made by Boeing led to this problem.

The first decision was to use more powerful engines than those on the 737 without undergoing a new certification process.

The second decision was to reposition the engines, which threw the aircraft off balance and caused it to pitch up. To address this issue, MCAS technology was added to bring this model’s handling characteristics closer to those of earlier 737s.

The third decision was that MCAS was omitted from the flight manuals; as a result, pilots were unaware of this system and therefore did not necessarily adjust their flying accordingly.
